A · The task, and the two ways LF can help

Every family answers the same question: given a small condition vector X (the PDE's parameters), abundant cheap low-fidelity fields (coarse-grid solves), and a handful of expensive high-fidelity fields, predict the HF field for a new X. There are exactly two places the LF data can enter, and this single design choice splits the whole zoo:

B · The shared FNO backbone — read once, reused by 24 of 30 families

Every FNO family vendors a near-identical copy of this network (cosmetic diffs only). Defaults everywhere: 64 hidden channels, 4 blocks, 12 Fourier modes per axis, working grid = HF grid capped at 256 px. Understand this once and every family below reduces to "same trunk, different fusion".

Sharp-field outlook (applies to every FNO family): blocks 4-5 are the whole story. A shock's spectrum decays as k⁻¹, so deleting modes >12 floors the achievable error at 3-10% rel-L2 regardless of training; the 1×1 bypass can pass sharp content through but cannot spatially reposition it. Fixes = more modes (factorized), a local conv path, or a different basis — report Part 4, P1.
Audit note: all resampling in the shared loaders is bilinear with antialias=False — LF fields upsampled to the working grid arrive pre-blurred, and >256 grids (era5 721×1440 → 128×256) are downsampled with aliasing. Training loss is plain MSE in scaler-normalized space in every FNO family (Transolver/v9 add a rel-L2 term).

C1 · Transfer-learning FNO — the current winners (#1, #2, #8, #10)

Multi-fidelity lives in the training schedule, not the architecture: one FNO, trained twice. Stage 1 fits the abundant LF fields (lr 1e-3, full epoch budget); stage 2 re-fits all weights on the scarce HF fields (fresh optimizer, lr 3e-4, full epoch budget). The LF stage is scaffolding: it moves the weights into a basin where "solutions of this PDE family" are easy, so the few HF samples only need to nudge, not teach from scratch.

② in detail — what "fine-tune" actually does added after your annotation

After stage 1, the optimizer is discarded but every weight is kept. A fresh AdamW starts at lr 3e-4 (⅓ of stage 1's 1e-3), cosine-annealed toward zero over the full epoch budget, and trains on only the scarce HF pairs (X → HF field, MSE in scaler-normalized space). Nothing is frozen — all ~4.7M weights may move — but the small learning rate and few gradient steps mean they move only slightly: the network already predicts plausible coarse solutions from stage 1, so the updates adjust fine detail rather than relearning from scratch. The LF data never appears in stage 2 — its entire influence survives through the initialization. That is the whole multi-fidelity mechanism of this family: with a handful of HF samples, training from random init would badly overfit, but starting inside the LF-shaped basin means a nearby low-error solution exists and few samples suffice to reach it. The lower lr is the guard against catastrophic forgetting; the fresh optimizer (rather than continuing stage 1's) avoids inheriting stale momentum/second-moment statistics tuned to the LF loss surface. Audit caveat: mf_fno_transfer_bar runs stage 2 for the fixed epoch count with no validation split or early stopping.

FiLM in depth — channels vs blocks, and what γ / β actually do added after your annotation

Channels vs blocks are the two axes of the network. A block is a stage in depth: this FNO is 4 blocks in sequence, and the data passes through block 1 → 2 → 3 → 4, each one round of "mix globally (SpectralConv), adjust pointwise (1×1), normalize, bend (GELU)". A channel is one slot of width: between blocks the data is a tensor of shape (batch, 64, H, W) — 64 parallel H×W feature maps, like a 64-color version of RGB, except the network invents what each "color" means (one channel might carry boundary-layer-ness, another local curvature…). So: 64 channels wide, 4 blocks deep.

What FiLM computes. At every block, a small MLP reads the condition vector X (~3 numbers) and outputs 128 numbers: a scale γ = (γ₁…γ₆₄) and a shift β = (β₁…β₆₄) — one pair per channel. After the block's GroupNorm, channel c's entire H×W map is transformed as z_c → γ_c(X) · z_c + β_c(X). The same γ_c, β_c applies at every pixel of that channel — that is why it is called feature-wise linear modulation: it modulates whole features, not individual pixels.

What γ and β do, intuitively. Think of a 64-track mixing desk. γ is the volume fader per track: γ_c ≈ 2 says "for this PDE setting, feature c matters — turn it up"; γ_c ≈ 0 says "mute it for this sample"; negative flips its sign. β is the trim/offset per track: it shifts the feature's baseline, which matters because GELU comes next — adding a positive β pushes a channel into GELU's pass-through zone (feature flows on), a negative β pushes it toward the dead zone (feature suppressed). Together, γ picks how much of each learned feature to use and β picks whether it clears the nonlinearity — and because each of the 4 blocks has its own FiLM MLP, the mix is re-tuned at every depth.

Why this beats concat (#8 → #1 with no other change). Concat hands X to the network once, as 3 extra input channels; after four blocks of mixing, the conditioning must have survived the entire depth, and it can only ever act additively as more input. FiLM re-injects X at every block with a direct multiplicative handle on all 64 features — a much shorter path, and a more expressive one (rescaling behavior vs adding data). The FiLM MLPs are zero-initialized, so at the start γ = 1, β = 0 and the network is exactly the unconditioned FNO — conditioning is grown during training rather than destabilizing it. Cost: ~4.3k parameters per block, a rounding error on 4.7M.

Precise placement in this repo: FiLMNorm = GroupNorm with its usual fixed per-channel affine removed, replaced by the X-dependent γ, β — i.e. the normalization's knobs, steered by the PDE parameters instead of frozen after training. Variant: fno_coreg_conditioned plays the identical trick with the fidelity index m instead of X. In mf_fno_transfer_film, FiLM sees X only — never m — so the multi-fidelity mechanism stays purely in the two-stage schedule.

C2 · FIRE — distribution-conditioned residual correction (#3-#15, #24)

The zoo's novel contribution (field analog of the FIRE tabular method, arXiv:2601.22371). Core bet: where the LF model is uncertain is where the LF→HF gap is large, so don't just feed the correction network the LF mean — feed it the LF model's whole predictive distribution, summarized as five per-pixel fields. Eleven variants share this pipeline and differ in exactly one place: how the LF distribution is obtained.

Sharp-field outlook: the additive anchor μ carries the mispositioned LF shock, and δ must build a dipole through 12 modes. FIRE's hedge — σ lighting up where the shock might be — only works if ensemble members disagree; a systematic coarse-solver bias (1st-order Godunov always smears the same way) leaves σ silent exactly where it is needed. Gaussian-collapse variants (mdn/laplace/dkl) additionally cannot represent bimodal position ambiguity at all.

The shared training loop draws a fresh random permutation every mini-batch (samples can repeat/skip within an "epoch").

C3 · Coregionalization — one net for all fidelities, modulated by m (#13, #19-#21)

IFC-style (Li 2022): a single network is trained on all fidelities jointly, with a continuous fidelity index m ∈ [0,1] telling it which rung of the ladder to produce. LF data enters as extra supervision at other m values — at test time only X and m=1 are consumed. The three in-checkout variants differ in where m modulates the network.

C5 · Attention / Transolver — the only families that consume real LF fields at test time (#23, #26, #30)

These are point-cloud transformers, not FNOs: fields become sets of tokens (coordinates + values), and fidelities exchange information by cross-attention. No Fourier truncation anywhere — but all cross-fidelity information must squeeze through a small number of learned slice tokens (soft groupings of points), which is a different bottleneck. C6 · Vision-foundation features — an honest negative (#16 + one unbenchmarked sibling)

Hypothesis: a frozen vision foundation model (DINOv2) looking at the LF field as an image might extract structure (vortices, fronts) that helps predict the HF field. Both variants predict the LF field from X with one FNO, encode that prediction with frozen DINOv2 into a single 768-d CLS vector (computed once, offline), and condition a second FNO's correction on it.

The four takeaways the numbers support

  1. Conditioning mechanism > extra architecture — concat→FiLM alone is #8→#1.
  2. Known physics adds a little more — the PINN term gives the best median (0.0122).
  3. LF uncertainty is a real signal — FIRE clusters #3-#15; but cheap uncertainty (mcdropout #24) is worse than none (additive #18).
  4. Classic correction rules and external wrappers trail on these (smooth) datasets.

Caveat for the upcoming SURF-2026 datasets

This entire ranking was measured on mostly-smooth fields — FNO's comfort zone. The companion report argues from theory, benchmarks, and the code audit that the order should roughly hold on Kuramoto-Sivashinsky and invert on the Euler-shock and Cahn-Hilliard datasets, and proposes nine new families (local paths, warp-then-correct registration, band-splitting, level-set heads…) built for that regime.

E · Glossary — the recurring vocabulary

Fidelity index m ∈ [0,1]. Dataset-assigned label for each rung of the fidelity ladder (0 = coarsest, 1 = HF). Correlated with grid resolution but not computed from it — the values (e.g. {0, 0.143, 0.429, 1}) are given by the dataset, and fidelity in general can mean solver tolerance or timestep, not just pixels. Networks consume it via MLPs on [m, m²].

Condition vector X. The PDE's parameters for one sample (~3-10 scalars: coefficients, boundary values, quadrant states…). The only test-time input that every family receives.

FiLM. Feature-wise Linear Modulation: a small MLP maps a conditioner (X or m) to per-channel scale γ and shift β applied after normalization in every block. Always zero-initialized here (γ=1, β=0 at start) so training begins from the unconditioned network.

SpectralConv2d. FFT → keep the lowest 12×12 (16 or 20 in two families) frequency modes → multiply by learned complex weights → inverse FFT. The global mixing operation that makes an FNO an operator — and the low-pass filter that caps its sharpness.

Per-fidelity scaler. scaler[m] = max|Y_train at m|; training happens on Y/scaler, predictions are re-multiplied. Fixes ~40× magnitude gaps between rungs (ifc_poisson).

Working grid. The HF native grid, capped at 256 px on the longest side; everything is bilinearly resampled onto it.

Elo / med relL2 / composite_nRMSE. Elo = pairwise per-dataset win-rate ranking (robust to blow-ups); med relL2 = median per-dataset relative-L2; composite_nRMSE = geomean over datasets of best-per-dataset nRMSE (the factory's optimization target). All are built on the same per-sample relative-L2.

ifc_raw / npz_l*. The two dataset storage formats (loader families). Note: ifc_raw test splits are HF-only, which forces the LF-consuming families to synthesize test-time LF by downsampling HF (see audit).

Smoke vs full. Smoke = 200 epochs on ifc_heat + ifc_poisson (the per-cycle gate, ≤30 min/H100); full = 2500 epochs × 17 datasets (the leaderboard).
