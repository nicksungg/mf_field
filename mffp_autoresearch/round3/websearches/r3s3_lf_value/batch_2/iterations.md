### iteration_1 — field framing: data-quantity scaling for PDE surrogates
*Rationale:* the batch-2 direction is a **quantity** law, not an architecture; establish whether LF-sample-count learning curves are a published genre.
Terms: (1) `neural operator scaling law number of training samples power law parametric PDE surrogate data efficiency`; (2) `multi-fidelity optimal sample allocation how many low-fidelity samples per high-fidelity sample surrogate accuracy`; (3) `learning curve number of low-fidelity training samples diminishing returns knee multi-fidelity neural network sweep`.
Findings: data-efficiency for NOs is active but framed as *architecture/active-learning* wins, not LF-count laws — GNS reaches <1 % rel-L2 with 30/1000 trajectories [https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06154v1 — **fetched**]; power-law fits are over *parameters*, not data [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15793 — search return]. MF allocation is a classic MFMC variance-minimisation problem with **no universal LF:HF ratio** [search returns: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1474034625009693, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.11708]. Crossover/knee behaviour is reported (MF≈SF at large n; MF wins only when HF is scarce) [https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2632-2153/adc222 — search return, **body blocked by bot wall**].
*Interpretation:* the genre exists; nothing yet ties LF **count** to a mediating learned quantity, and nothing removes LF from the test path.

### iteration_2 — exchange rate, coverage-vs-regularisation, count sweeps
Terms: (1) `effective sample size of low-fidelity data equivalent number of high-fidelity samples multi-fidelity surrogate exchange rate`; (2) `low-fidelity samples at new parameter locations improve surrogate by covering parameter space rather than regularization ablation`; (3) `number of low-fidelity training points sweep accuracy saturates multi-fidelity deep learning`.
Findings: **no** "effective sample size / exchange rate" formalism retrieved for MF surrogates (search engine itself flagged the gap). Coverage language is standard: "multi-fidelity data provide broad coverage of the admissible parameter space, while high-fidelity samples are more sparsely distributed" [https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17061v1 — **fetched**, MF-ROM for high-dimensional inputs]. **Decisive count-sweep hit** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.01699 — **fetched (pypdf, 20 pp)**]: "We will keep the number of high-fidelity training points fixed at 50 and we will vary the number of low-fidelity training points used"; MF-GP-nlow250/500/1000 MAE **1.46 / 0.828 / 0.866 %**, max err 7.12 / 3.75 / 3.55; "Increasing the number of low-fidelity training points from 250 to 500 reduced the mean and maximum error. However, increasing this to 1000 ... did not increase accuracy ... This is because the number of high-fidelity training points is fixed."
*Interpretation:* the **LF-count sweep with a saturation knee at fixed N_hf is published** — on a scalar QoI, with LF in the fusion path.
