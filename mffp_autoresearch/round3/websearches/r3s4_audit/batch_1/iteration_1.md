# Iteration 1 — r3s4_audit / batch 1

## Search rationale

B1 is pre-directed: certify the repaired-panel **mce** and the **floors**. Round-2 r2s4-B1 already established that "MCE from a 3-seed spread" is `preempted` (Agarwal precipice, Du +1%), so re-searching that exact framing would waste the loop. What round 3 adds is (i) a *second*, independent noise source — the copy-LF **denominator** is itself an estimated per-cell quantity with ADR r3-0003 `cell_stability` bootstrap CIs — and (ii) the whole **benchmark-integrity / estimator-integrity instrumentation** layer (`preflight.py` five-defect checks + `data_binding.py`). So iteration 1 probes three fronts: the meteorological skill-score literature (the oldest home of "score normalized by a reference forecast", and thus of denominator pathology), the ML benchmark-integrity/leakage-audit literature, and the minimum-detectable-effect / benchmark-power literature.

### Term 1 — skill score / reference denominator

Top results (WebSearch): Necker et al. 2024 "The fractions skill score for ensemble forecast verification" (https://rmets.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/qj.4824, preprint https://www.techrxiv.org/users/650600/articles/659203/master/file/data/Necker_et_al_2024_preprint_R1_pFSS/Necker_et_al_2024_preprint_R1_pFSS.pdf); "How to Derive Skill from the Fractions Skill Score", Mon. Wea. Rev. 153(6) 2025 (https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/mwre/153/6/MWR-D-24-0120.1.xml); GMD 2025 "Decomposition of skill scores for conditional verification" (https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/18/361/2025/); NOAA SWPC Forecast Verification Glossary (https://www.swpc.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/images/u30/Forecast%20Verification%20Glossary.pdf).

The search engine's own summary of the MWR 2025 paper states the key point directly: *"A new reference score scales appropriately with the neighborhood size and is mathematically consistent, in contrast to the existing reference score, which is derived at the grid scale only and uses inconsistent terms in the numerator and denominator"*, and that the deterministic FSS *"is often undefined for pathological cases where one or both of the forecast and observations are 0"* and *"becomes problematic for very large biases in the event frequency"*.

**Fetch (bash/curl, JS-free extraction) of https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/mwre/153/6/MWR-D-24-0120.1.xml** returned the landing page and full reference list but not the paywalled body (LEN=141819, body text not exposed). What it *does* confirm, with URLs, is the lineage this project's copy-LF skill sits in: **Murphy, A. H., 1988, "Skill scores based on the mean square error and their relationships to the correlation coefficient", Mon. Wea. Rev. 116, 2417–2424, https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(1988)116<2417:SSBOTM>2.0.CO;2**, and **Mittermaier, M. P., 2021, "A 'meta' analysis of the fractions skill score: the limiting case and implications for aggregation", Mon. Wea. Rev. 149, 3491–3504, https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-18-0106.1** — an entire paper on *how a skill score's limiting/degenerate cases distort aggregation*, i.e. the published analogue of our `degenerate_rows` + mean-of-ratios problem. Also in the list: Casati, Lussana & Crespi 2023, "Scale-separation diagnostics and the **symmetric bounded efficiency**" (an explicitly *bounded* reformulation of a skill score, motivated by unbounded/ill-behaved normalized scores).

Interpretation for us: MSE-based skill scores normalized by a reference are ~40 years old and their denominator pathologies are a named, published subfield. Our copy-LF skill is a Murphy-1988-class skill score with the reference forecast = "copy the coarse solve". Any B1 claim of novelty in *the idea of* auditing a skill-score denominator is dead on arrival.

### Term 2 — benchmark integrity / leakage audits

Top results: Roth 2026 "Which Leakage Types Matter? A Quantitative Landscape Across 2,047 Benchmark Datasets" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04199, pdf https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04199); "Benchmark Data Contamination of Large Language Models: A Survey" (https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04244v1); "Are Large Language Models Memorizing Bug Benchmarks?" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.13323); `leakr` R package (https://cherylisabella.r-universe.dev/leakr); Uplatz "The Integrity Crisis in Machine Learning" (https://uplatz.com/blog/the-integrity-crisis-in-machine-learning-a-comprehensive-report-on-data-contamination-detection-for-honest-benchmarking/).

**Fetch of https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04199** (~150 words): Roth runs 28 within-subject counterfactual experiments across 2,047 iid tabular datasets plus a boundary experiment on 129 temporal datasets to measure four leakage classes. Class I (estimation leakage — fitting scalers on full data) is *negligible*: all nine conditions give |ΔAUC| ≤ 0.005. **Class II (selection leakage — peeking, seed cherry-picking) is the dominant one: the measured effect is consistent with ~90% noise exploitation inflating reported scores.** Class III (memorization) scales with model capacity, d_z from 0.37 (Naive Bayes) to 1.11 (Decision Tree) at 10% duplication. Class IV (boundary leakage) is invisible under random CV. The headline: the textbook emphasis is inverted — normalization leakage matters least, **selection leakage at practical dataset sizes matters most**. This is a direct, citable quantitative justification for the mce: a certified minimum claimable effect is precisely the instrument that removes Class II seed cherry-picking, and the paper prices what happens without it.

The search engine also surfaced (search-result-level only, not fetched) the claim that a cross-field audit found 294→648 published papers invalidated by leakage across 17→30 fields, and that new ImageNet test sets produced 11–14 point accuracy drops. Treat as unverified until fetched.

### Term 3 — minimum detectable effect / benchmark power

Top results: "The Sample Complexity of LLM Evaluation" (https://www.tmls.nyc/research/eval-sample-complexity); "Stop Guessing When to Stop Testing: Efficient Model Evaluation with Just Enough Data" (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08522); bootstrap-CI-for-metrics tutorial (https://medium.com/data-science/get-confidence-intervals-for-any-model-performance-metrics-in-machine-learning-f9e72a3becb2).

**Fetch of https://www.tmls.nyc/research/eval-sample-complexity** (~150 words): the paper's framing is exactly B1's — *"a benchmark score is a point estimate of a population quantity, and whether it can distinguish one model from another is a question of statistical power, not of coverage"*. Contributions: small-n interval estimation (Wald undercovers below a few hundred items; Wilson / Clopper-Pearson / Jeffreys repairs, coverage computed exactly from the binomial); **the paired design** (McNemar variance from a covariance decomposition; pairing cancels shared item difficulty and saves 30–60% of samples as a function of cross-model error correlation); **clustering and effective sample size** (survey-sampling design effect in closed form; law of total variance separating item noise from generation/seed noise — *"which explains why seed averaging raises power"*); a **minimum-detectable-effect atlas** showing many reported gaps sit inside their own suite's noise floor (worked MDEs given in the search summary: HumanEval MDE 6.9 so a 2-point gain is unresolvable; SWE-bench Verified MDE 4.0 so a 1-point gain is unresolvable); and a closed-form sizing recipe with multiple-comparisons correction. It cites Card et al. 2020 (NLP chronically underpowered; a 2,000-sentence MT test set has ~75% power for 1 BLEU) and Bouthillier et al. MLSys 2021 (multiple variance sources; a single-seed number understates variance). Notably it separates **power from validity** in its limitations section — the same split this stream needs (mce = power; preflight = validity).

## Interpretation

The *idea* of a certified minimum-detectable effect for a benchmark is thoroughly published and now has a 2026 atlas paper with explicit machinery (paired design, design-effect/ESS, law-of-total-variance seed decomposition) that is strictly better than a bare 3-seed max−min spread. Separately, skill-score denominator pathology is a 40-year-old meteorology subfield with a dedicated paper on how limiting cases distort aggregation. Neither is novel; both are *adoptable*, and the honest B1 framing is "instrument certification using published machinery", not "new method".
