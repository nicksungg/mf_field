## Findings

### Term 1 — per-sample relative-error aggregation

Top results: Chu, Liu, Biswas & Shen 2026, "Do Physics Foundation Models Learn Generalizable Physics? **A Bias-Aware Benchmark** Across Physical Regimes and Distribution Shifts" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29283, html https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29283v2); "PDEAgent-Bench" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09636); "Forecast Error Measures: Scaled, Relative, and other Errors" (https://deep-and-shallow.com/2020/10/01/forecast-error-measures-scaled-relative-and-other-errors/).

**Fetch of https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29283v2** (targeted extraction, ~150 words): the benchmark spans 8 physical dynamics × 3 training mixtures × 25 test regimes (ID / shift / OOD), 5 physics-foundation-model architectures × 4 variants = 60,000 measurements, and its stated motivation is that *"evaluations often collapse performance into a single average score under a fixed training distribution"*. Its metric section states verbatim: *"Our base metric is per-sample-frame relative L2 error, averaged over…"* and *"Computing the relative error per sample and frame before aggregation avoids allowing high-energy trajectories, or frames with larger absolute field magnitude, to dominate the metric."* So the **mean-of-per-sample-ratios choice is published, named, and justified for exactly our reason** (preventing high-magnitude samples from dominating). What this source does *not* do is audit the residual failure mode of that choice — that a per-sample ratio with a near-zero denominator (or a near-zero *reference gap*, our `degenerate_rows` case) then dominates in the opposite direction. The blog source (https://deep-and-shallow.com/2020/10/01/forecast-error-measures-scaled-relative-and-other-errors/) confirms the standard forecasting remedies are trimming, median aggregation (MdAPE), and Winsorizing — i.e. our ADR r3-0003 D1 trim is the textbook remedy, not an invention.

### Term 2 — mandatory floor / trivial-baseline standards in SciML

Top results: McGreivy & Hakim, "Weak baselines and reporting biases lead to overoptimism in machine learning for fluid-related partial differential equations" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.07218 — already binding from round-2 B1); Wyder et al. 2025, "**Common Task Framework** For a Critical Evaluation of Scientific Machine Learning Algorithms", NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks (https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23166); "An Extensive Benchmark for Scientific Machine Learning", NeurIPS 2022 D&B (https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/0a9747136d411fb83f0cf81820d44afb-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf); "Divide-and-Conquer Modeling for the CTF-4-Science Lorenz Benchmark" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10084).

**Fetch of https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23166** (~150 words): Wyder et al. (14 authors incl. Cranmer, Kutz) argue that SciML's rapid development *"has outpaced the creation of standardized, objective benchmarks — leading to weak baselines, reporting bias, and inconsistent evaluations across methods,"* which *"undermines reproducibility, misguides resource allocation, and obscures scientific progress."* Their remedy is a Common Task Framework: a curated dataset set plus **task-specific metrics** spanning forecasting, state reconstruction and generalization *under realistic constraints including noise and limited data*, benchmarked first on Kuramoto–Sivashinsky and Lorenz, then a sea-surface-temperature competition with a **true holdout**. Long-term vision: *"replace ad hoc comparisons with standardized evaluations on hidden test sets."* Crucially, the CTF's answer to weak baselines is **hidden test sets and standardized tasks** — a governance mechanism — **not** a certified panel of training-free trivial predictors reported as mandatory columns. That distinction is the same one round-2 B1 recorded, now reconfirmed against a 2025 NeurIPS-track source.

### Term 3 — data↔artifact binding / provenance hashing

Top results: Pocock 2021, "Tribuo: Machine Learning with Provenance in Java" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03022); "OriginBlame: Record- and Token-Level Data Provenance for AI Training Datasets" (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13037v1); US patent 10802822 "System and method for reproducible machine learning" (https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10802822); US patent 12079699 "Verifying the provenance of a machine learning system" (https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12079699); practitioner guide (https://inferensys.com/guides/explainability-and-traceability-for-high-risk-ai/setting-up-a-data-provenance-system-for-training-datasets).

**Fetch of https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03022** (~150 words): Tribuo's abstract diagnoses precisely the hole `data_binding.py` closes: *"Current ML monitoring systems often provide provenance and experiment tracking as a **layer on top of** an ML library, allowing room for imperfect tracking and **skew between the tracked object and the metadata**."* Tribuo's fix is to make provenance intrinsic — *"All Tribuo's models and evaluations record the full processing pipeline for input data, along with the training algorithms, hyperparameters and data transformation steps automatically. The provenance lives inside the model object."* The search engine additionally reports (result-level, from the reproducible-ML patent) that standard hash sets in reproducible ML are *"data path hashes, source code hashes, hyperparameter hashes, and environment hashes"* and that *"before model training, verifying the hash of a prepared dataset matches the hash logged in the provenance system ensures no corruption."* Conclusion: content-hash-gating a cache against dataset bytes is **established engineering practice with a patent trail** — zero novelty available here.

## Interpretation

Two of the three B1 ingredients are now firmly `preempted` at the mechanism level: per-sample relative-error aggregation (with the anti-domination rationale stated verbatim) and dataset-hash provenance gating. The floor-panel question reconfirms round-2's answer from a stronger source: the SciML community's published fix for weak baselines is hidden test sets and task-specific metrics (CTF), not a mandatory training-free floor column — so the composition remains open while the *concern* is fully preempted.
