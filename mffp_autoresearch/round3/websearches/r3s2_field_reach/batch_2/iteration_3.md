# Iteration 3 — approximability-limited vs information-limited residuals, and MF correction under a spectral prior

## Search rationale

Two of the three batch-2 candidate directions rest on the claim that the surviving `cahn_hilliard` gap is **approximability**, not information: B1's model-free probe says the nearest condition-space neighbour's HF field is 98 % as different as a random pair's, so at the available sampling density the condition->field map is effectively non-smooth. Before the brainstormer builds a card on that reading, I need to know (a) whether "slow n-width / non-smooth parameter-to-solution map" is the established name for it, (b) whether a *diagnostic* separating data-limited from approximability-limited surrogate error exists, and (c) whether spectrally-regularised MF residual correction with a Nyquist/coherence cutoff is published in the MF-neural-operator literature specifically.

## Search terms used

1. `Kolmogorov n-width slow decay parametric PDE surrogate approximability barrier versus limited training data sample complexity diagnostic`
2. `diagnose surrogate error limited data versus non-smooth parameter-to-solution map nearest neighbor distance in parameter space Lipschitz estimate`
3. `multi-fidelity residual correction spectral transfer function ridge regularization Nyquist cutoff coherence low-fidelity neural operator few high-fidelity samples`

## Findings

### Term 1 — Kolmogorov n-width is exactly the established vocabulary

Snippet-level but unambiguous and consistent across the corpus: *"The Kolmogorov n-width (KnW) is used to characterize the approximability of the solution manifold — the set of parameter-dependent solutions of the PDE — by a linear trial subspace"*; *"Non-affine parametric dependencies, nonlinearities and advection-dominated regimes can result in a slow Kolmogorov n-width decay, which precludes the realization of efficient reduced-order models based on linear subspace approximations"*. Sources (all **snippet-only**): <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.03117> (Kolmogorov widths and low-rank approximations of parametric elliptic PDEs), <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.01397> (nonlinear model reduction for transport-dominated problems), <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021999125001986> (DD-ROM + GNN for slow-KnW manifolds), <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.00360>, <https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333097038_Decay_of_the_Kolmogorov_N_-width_for_wave_problems>.

The fetched Duraisamy article from iteration 2 supplies the same framing in the surrogate-evaluation register and is the citable version: *"When the parameter-to-solution map is smooth and the solution set has low intrinsic dimension: rapidly decaying Kolmogorov n-width, any competent approximation method will succeed"* [<https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20061>, fetched iteration 2].

**Direct consequence for us**: the ac/ch contrast is a *slow-n-width vs benign-manifold* contrast in the literature's own words, and the ifc cells (oracle-affine residual 5.4e-16, ensemble rank 5) are the textbook "benign manifold" case that the same article says invalidates the benchmark cell as a test of anything but interpolation.

### Term 2 — no published nearest-neighbour-in-parameter diagnostic for this attribution

**No usable results for the diagnostic.** The corpus confirms the *phenomenon* — *"Due to the highly nonlinear nature of the parameter-to-solution map in even the simplest dynamical systems, constructed surrogate models are often inaccurate"* (<https://arxiv.org/html/2310.10831>, snippet-only); *"Challenges ... include the high dimensionality of the parameter space and possible non-smooth dependence of the quantity of interest on the parameter"* (<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.07023>, snippet-only) — but the engine stated outright that the results *"don't specifically discuss the nearest neighbor distance metrics or Lipschitz estimates"*. Other snippet-only returns: <https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22654v1> (parameter-conditioned U-Net surrogate), <https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18012v1> (neural shape operator expression-rate bounds).

The nearest published *diagnostic* remains batch 1's find, [Chen] "Diagnosing the conditional-mean barrier in scientific machine-learning surrogates" <https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28076> (fetched in `batch_1/iteration_4.md`), which separates *deterministic underfitting* from *irreducible conditional variability* — a different cut than data-limited vs approximability-limited.

### Term 3 — MF residual correction with a spectral/band prior (FETCHED x2)

- [Villatoro, Geraci & Schiavazzi 2025] "Assessing the performance of correlation-based multi-fidelity neural emulators" — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02868> / <https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02868v1> — **FETCHED**. This is the closest published object to the r3s2 route contrast. Verbatim: the study spans *"exact or learnable low-fidelity information, and for varying training dataset size"*, and *"We further analyze the added value of the multi-fidelity approach by conducting equivalent single-fidelity tests for each case, quantifying the performance gains achieved through fusing multiple sources of information."* Protocol, verbatim: *"The single-fidelity networks use identical architectures to their multi-fidelity counterparts but are trained exclusively on the high-fidelity data without access to low-fidelity information."* Result, verbatim: *"multi-fidelity approaches consistently outperform their single-fidelity counterparts, particularly in data-scarce scenarios, although the magnitude of improvement depends on both the problem characteristics and the chosen architecture."* Caveats for us: their outputs are low-/high-dimensional **functions of parameters** (1-D/2-D test functions, a reaction-diffusion PDE), the **exact LF model is callable at test time**, there is no spectral-band accounting, and "learnable LF" means an LF network trained on abundant LF data — the architecture-matched single-vs-multi-fidelity comparison, however, is exactly the arm structure a "route contrast" would use.
- [Duba-Sullivan et al., IEEE SoutheastCon 2026] "ResSR: A Computationally Efficient Residual Approach to Super-Resolving Multispectral Images" — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13225> — **FETCHED**. Verbatim: *"It then applies a residual correction step to restore low-frequency spatial consistency while preserving high-frequency detail recovered by the spectral reconstruction"*, with the whole method *"without supervised training or spatially-coupled optimization"*. Band-partitioned residual correction — correct one spectral range, keep the other — is established practice outside SciML.

Snippet-only from the same term: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10958> (physics-guided MF KAN: *"the physics-guided network receives the low-fidelity coefficients, predicts the residual relative to high-fidelity data, and reconstructs the high-fidelity coefficients"*); <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0141029625016098> (pretrain-finetune neural operator for MF surrogates); <https://arxiv.org/html/2407.15110> (practical MF ML, deterministic+Bayesian fusion); <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30059> (ridge regression from Poisson resetting — spectral regularisation, generic).

**In-repo prior websearch (cite, do not re-derive)**: `docs/reports/MF_Sharp_HighFreq_Report.md` lines 224–230 / 293–297 already record the coherence-gated band-split ("hard-constrain the output's Fourier coefficients below a per-dataset cutoff k_c ... estimate k_c ... as the wavenumber where LF/HF cross-spectral coherence drops below ~0.7"), and `MF_Leaderboard_Beaters_2026_Report.md` line 239 records that correlation-based fusion degrades "as HF shrinks or LF is biased".

## Interpretation

The approximability reading of `cahn_hilliard` has an established name (slow Kolmogorov n-width / non-smooth parameter-to-solution map) and an established evaluation consequence (Duraisamy's "if a classical reduced model matches you, the benchmark tests interpolation"), so B2 may **use** the framing but cannot claim it; what is still unretrievable is a *nearest-neighbour-in-condition sampling-density* diagnostic that separates data-limited from approximability-limited error on a **field** target. Direction 1's ingredients (ridge/shrinkage, band-limiting, coherence cutoffs, band-partitioned residual correction) are each published, and 2512.02868 now supplies a published architecture-matched single- vs multi-fidelity arm protocol — which sharply narrows what a route contrast can claim.

`ENOUGH` on field context: three iterations have produced an established name, an established evaluation standard and an established arm protocol for every candidate direction. Iterations 4–5 go entirely to §3.3 refutation searches.
