MFFP autoresearch · mentor update

Rounds 1–2: results, benchmark findings, and a live round-2 launch

2026-07-31 · Eloise Zeng · sources: round1/docs/round1_report.md, round2/program.md, per-experiment cards

The program runs in two regimes. Round 1: the physics solver is allowed at test time — a model receives the cheap coarse-grid solve (the LF field) plus a small condition vector, and its job is to correct that solve toward the fine-grid truth. Round 2 removes the solver at test time — the model gets the condition vector alone and must predict the fine-grid field directly; LF fields exist only as a training signal. Round 2 is the deployment case where a company hands over historical simulation data but no callable solver.

Round 1 is closed and both success criteria were met. The champion model beats copying the low-fidelity field by 8.1× on the sharp-dataset panel, and the round surfaced benchmark-integrity defects that change how every prior skill number should be read. The top-3 slate's seed-confirmation runs are in the SLURM queue. Round 2 launched this morning with floors re-certified before any model ran, and its first mechanism analysis already produced the round's defining finding.

1 · How the autoresearch system works

The system is a no-oracle research harness: a persistent orchestrator drives parallel research streams, each running batches of single-slot experiments through a fixed pipeline of specialized agents. Every experiment lives in its own git worktree and an append-only experiment card that records hypothesis, design, pre-registered falsification clause, recipe, results, and mechanism analysis.

Vocabulary: stream · card · arm · architecture

TermWhat it isExample
Stream A research question, run as a sequence of experiments s4_hybrid_routing
Card {stream}-B{batch} One experiment: hypothesis, pre-registered falsification clause, and a code build. One card = one worktree = one SLURM job s4-B3 = 3rd experiment of s4
Arm One runnable configuration of that build — the unit that gets a skill score. The treatment (the idea under test) plus the controls/ablations needed to interpret it, all trained in the same job so comparisons are paired. Count varies with the hypothesis (4–7 in round 1; diagnostics have 0) s4-B3's five: dc_cleaned (treatment), dc_raw (no cleaning), lsi_alone (no network at all), router, router_forced_wrong
Architecture A reusable ingredient inside arms — not what s*-B* names. The same backbone appears across many cards; sibling arms may differ architecturally or not at all FNO+FiLM backbone; the ~85k CNN corrector (used by s6-B1, s6-B2, s4-B3)
Model identity Stream-batch + arm — provenance, like "Table 2, row 3 of study X" "s4-B3 dc_cleaned"

The brainstormer proposes a card's treatment and its arms in one act, novelty-checked against the websearcher's verdict — new "models" are usually novel compositions of proven ingredients, since an architecture without an interpretable contrast is tuning, which the program bans. Cards mark each arm's role (PRIMARY vs control), and the winner isn't always the treatment: s1-B3's control self_only beat its gain-head treatment and became the criterion-1 entry — recorded as a surprise and attributed by the follow-up experiment. s4-B3 is the opposite story: the treatment won, and the arm contrast is precisely how "the gain is the cleaning stage alone; routing adds zero" became a conclusion rather than a guess.

  1. Web-searcher — retrieval-grounded prior-art verdict on the candidate direction (novel / preempted / open), max 5 search iterations.
  2. Brainstormer — designs exactly one experiment, with a complete training recipe and a falsification clause that must clear the certified noise floor. No post-hoc goalposts.
  3. Starter — creates the worktree and transcribes the proposal verbatim into the card.
  4. Builder — implements the model family in the worktree, smoke-tests, commits atomically.
  5. Code reviewer — adversarial PASS / SUGGEST / FAIL gate before any GPU time is spent; reviewers can never submit jobs.
  6. Orchestrator — the only actor that submits to SLURM. A debugger agent handles failures: 5 algorithmic retries then auto-skip; infra failures don't count.
  7. Initial analyzer — parses result JSONs only (never recomputes metrics), fills per-dataset skill tables, applies validity gates.
  8. Mechanism analyzer — three iterative probe turns answering why; generalizable probes get promoted into a shared tools library for later experiments.

Guard rails: one hashed nRMSE definition (results with mismatched hashes refuse to compare), frozen certified noise floors and anchors, immutable card fields, report-only guard datasets, checkpoint-resume everywhere because the partition is preemptable. A maintainer agent on a cron keeps the dashboard and a timing ledger.

Round-1 scale: 7 streams · 21 cards (17 model, 4 diagnostic) · ~55 SLURM jobs · 12 ADRs · ~20 promoted probe tools — in about 3 days, with operator gates (my explicit go) on seed confirms and full runs.

2 · What we benchmark against — and why

The objective is the geometric mean of skill over a 6-dataset critical panel, where skill = nRMSE(model) / nRMSE(reference), lower is better. The panel was selected adversarially, from data: before the round, a 45-family model zoo existed, fully benchmarked at 2500 epochs, and its champion (mf_fno_transfer_film) loses to the trivial copy-LF baseline on every one of these six datasets (skill > 1, last column below). The panel is precisely the frontier where the existing SOTA fails — anything the zoo already solved would only reward tuning, which the program defines as not-research. The geomean means a model can't buy the panel by cratering one dataset, and per-dataset tables are mandatory next to every geomean.

Guard set (heat_local, fluid, sharp__sod_1d) — datasets the zoo already handles, run cheaply alongside any claimed panel win. A >2× regression flags the card for interpretation (never auto-reject): it catches models that win the sharp panel by breaking the easy cases. Datasets like era5 exist in the repo but are deliberately excluded — the panel is not "all our data," it's the certified frontier.

The adversarial choice paid off twice: probing why models lost to copy-LF is what exposed the benchmark's own defects (registration, wrap-seam, the helmholtz zero-predictor). Round 2 keeps the same six datasets for continuity, but re-derives every copy-LF denominator under the corrected reference construction and re-certified the noise floors before any experiment ran — skill < 1 in round 2 means beating an honestly-measured solver run.

What mechanistically worked

The round-best gain is the cleaning stage alone. The router/trust-head machinery built on top contributed exactly 0.000000: cleaning nests the correction library, so routing headroom collapses to zero. A two-sided causal probe showed the cleaning stage's eligibility decider is boundary-condition match — decidable before training with a training-free audit (mirror-extension refit recovers −38.9% on the mirror-BC dataset, costs +1840%/+7167% on periodic ones).

Benchmark-integrity findings (the part most relevant to the benchmark paper)

  • Registration defect: the LF→HF interpolation carries a (r−1)/2 half-cell shift; closed form derived. ~96–100% of sharp-panel "wins" were re-learned registration. Copy-LF denominators inflated 2.0–8.6×.
  • Wrap-seam defect in the periodic reference construction.
  • Helmholtz zero-predictor: the zero field (skill 3.0) beats every trained model; dataset stays report-only until it gets a zero-floor column.
  • Nested-ladder degeneracy (we could not find this reported anywhere): with aligned/nested fidelity ladders, all-pairs multi-fidelity training degenerates to top-rung replication.
  • ifc_poisson ladder is unpaired (found during round 2, independently by two streams): no shared condition vectors across rungs — paired-ladder semantics were never valid on that dataset. Generator-side (mffp_sharp/common/ladder.py), flagged for your call.

4 · Round 2 — launched today (condition vector → HF, no solver at test)

Models see LF fields only during training; at test they get the condition vector alone — the stripped data view physically omits test LF files. This matches the deployment case where a company hands over historical simulation data but no callable solver. Skill < 1 now means: beats actually running the LF solver and copying its output, under corrected denominators (registration and wrap-seam fixes landed in round2/eval/ before any experiment ran; r1 and r2 numbers are not comparable).

Early round-defining finding (r2s1 mechanism turn 1): per dataset, only 1–3 orthogonal field modes are predictable from the condition vector at all (out-of-fold R² > 0.1). Direct decoders are information-limited, not representation-limited — the panel splits into coefficient-unidentifiable datasets (1–2 decades of error is missing information) and basis-inadequate ones. On allen_cahn the entire learnable map is one scalar, which a 16M-parameter decoder overfits to rediscover.

5 · How the harness is performing

  • Throughput: idea → prior-art check → build → adversarial review → GPU run → analyzed card in roughly a day; round 2 went from launch commit to two analyzed experiments in one day. A timing ledger feeds better wall-clock requests (r2s1 ran 7 min against a 4 h ask).
  • The design catches things: pre-registration plus certified floors killed several would-be "wins" (claims inside noise are recorded, not celebrated); the reviewer gate caught confounds before GPU time; mechanism turns produce causal, reusable findings rather than leaderboard deltas.
  • Hazards managed: SLURM preemption (mandatory checkpoint-resume), session-restart replays (claim protocol with stage-file tags and pre-submit queue checks), GPU nondeterminism (~1.9e-4 relative envelope on H100 — bitwise gates are CPU-only by rule). The H100 queue backed up ~1 week, so all round-2 jobs moved to idle H200s (ADR r2-0004).
